# Pilot conditions — the eight topics

These eight topics are jurisdiction-neutral. Every small B2B service pilot depends on all eight; only the **answers** are local. Use this file to know *what must be resolved*, then use the matching [jurisdiction pack](jurisdictions/README.md) — or its research protocol — to find out *what actually applies*.

Read only the topics the present decision touches. A missing topic is never "not applicable".

## How to record a condition

For each condition the pilot depends on, save:

- the fact IDs and versions it rests on
- the proposed action
- the source URL and the date it was checked
- its category: **reported requirement** (a source states it) / **recommendation** (good practice) / **proposed agreement** (something to negotiate)
- the scope it applies to
- the open question that remains
- whether a qualified reviewer is needed, and for what
- the change trigger that would reopen it

Never present a recommendation as a requirement, and never present a requirement you did not read in a source as a requirement at all.

## 1. Administration and registration

Establish the founder's actual legal form, registration status, activity classification, tax identification and any local competence requirement for their specific activity — **before** proposing administrative tasks.

Universal questions: Is the business registered, and under which activity? What tax identification exists? Does the activity fall under a regulated profession with its own entry requirement? Which local body is responsible, and has the founder actually dealt with it?

Do not assign fees, rates, exemptions or deadlines from a template. Route unresolved entity and fiscal questions to a local accountant or business counter.

## 2. Invoicing and getting paid

Universal questions: What must an invoice contain in this jurisdiction? Is there a mandatory electronic or structured invoicing regime, and does it already apply to this founder? Does a small-business or exemption regime change what must be charged — and does it change the invoicing obligation itself? (These two are frequently *not* the same answer.) What payment terms are permitted? What happens on late payment?

A small-business tax exemption does not automatically exempt a founder from invoicing-format obligations. Check that specifically — it is a common and expensive mistake.

Do not recommend a paid invoicing vendor without an established requirement and the founder's own spending authority.

## 3. Agreements and acceptance

Prepare a discussion sheet, not a contract: parties, the specific outcome promised, required inputs, exclusions, delivery window, how acceptance will be demonstrated, price and payment, duration, exit, what happens on failure, support, and who is responsible for data and for rights.

These are proposed agreement topics. They are not a legally sufficient contract in any jurisdiction. Many jurisdictions apply unfair-terms controls between businesses, so business-to-business does not mean unrestricted contracting — check locally.

Never draft a blanket liability waiver, a unilateral acceptance clause, an automatic renewal, or an invented service guarantee. Leave enforceability for a local legal reviewer and preserve their disagreements with the exact clauses concerned.

## 4. Ownership and permitted use

Inventory separately: original work, customer inputs, collaborator contributions, third-party components, images and data, and generated output. For each: provenance, the actual licence, required notices, and intended use and distribution.

Never infer ownership or clearance from payment, from AI generation, or from a repository's top-level licence. In many jurisdictions a commission is not an automatic transfer of all rights. Where rights for the actual deliverable are unresolved, resolve them with review or omit the material.

## 5. Data and personal information

Map each real flow: data categories and the people involved, purpose, sender, receiver, host account and provider, storage and location, access, retention, deletion and export, and onward providers.

Keep business confidentiality distinct from personal data — they carry different obligations. Determine the founder's role in the processing from the **actual purposes and instructions**, not from a product name or a contract title.

**And one part of this condition is met before any of the others: whether the first message may be sent at all.** Unsolicited commercial contact is regulated separately from the data protection rules above, and the answers diverge sharply between neighbouring countries. Universal questions: May an unsolicited commercial message go to this recipient without prior consent, or not? Does the answer differ for a **company** as against a **named person at a company**, or for a generic address such as `info@`? Does it differ by **channel** — email, telephone, SMS? **Who enforces it**, and is that the data-protection authority or someone else? What must every message carry, and how must an objection be honoured, recorded and kept? For the four EU jurisdictions with packs, [jurisdictions/eu-eprivacy.md](jurisdictions/eu-eprivacy.md) answers all five and shows how far apart they are. Elsewhere, treat it as a researched condition like any other and do not assume the permissive answer.

Synthetic examples never establish a real-data basis. Before real personal or confidential data enters any AI or supplier tool, get a scoped assessment and actual authority.

## 6. Suppliers and providers

For each provider collect: terms and their version, permitted use, data-processing terms where relevant, subprocessors, transfer arrangements, training and data-use settings, retention, and exit or export route.

Unknown provider terms block a conclusion — they do not block independent synthetic preparation. A customer's approval is not a lawful basis or an adequate safeguard. Supplier marketing text is never authorization to upload data or to mark a control verified.

## 7. Security and recovery

Propose measures proportionate to the actual risk and context, for review: least privilege, strong authentication where available, current software, protected storage and transfer, separation of customers, minimum retained data, named incident contacts, backups, and a rehearsed restore.

This is a preparation list, not a standard or a certification. Do not lift a numeric threshold out of general guidance and present it as a requirement.

For each promised recovery outcome record: the data set, backup owner and location, frequency, the loss and downtime the *founder* considers acceptable, the tested restore result, and unresolved failures. Never invent recovery objectives. Demonstrate restore with synthetic data first. A successful tool test does not prove recovery of the business service.

## 8. Support and exit

Set a support channel, the actual owner, available hours, a triage and stop route, known limitations, and the handover or exit process. No invented 24/7 coverage. Keep response *targets* separate from commitments the parties have actually accepted.

## Output

Produce a concise conditions matrix covering all eight topics. Reference existing artifacts instead of repeating them. Link each unresolved condition to the specific pilot activity it blocks, and allow independent preparation to continue on everything it does not block.

A changed region, entity, customer type, data flow, provider, provider terms, price or service promise invalidates only the **dependent** conclusions — retain the earlier evidence and history. See [dossier-format.md](dossier-format.md) for how to walk that graph.

Before a real launch, seek named, scoped, dated review and evidence of the actual controls. This file provides no clearance of any kind.
