# Germany — jurisdiction pack

Scope: a solo founder established in Germany, selling a digital service to German business customers, no employees, small bounded pilot.

Out of scope and requiring separate research: consumer (B2C) sales, employees or subcontractors, regulated professions and the `Handwerksordnung` trades, cross-border supply, physical goods, payment or financial services, health data.

**This pack is preparation, not advice.** Consequential steps need a German `Steuerberater`; tax advice is a regulated activity in Germany and this pack is not it.

Verification convention: **[checked 2026-11-10]** means the source was fetched and read on that date. **[carried 2026-10-27, not refetched]** means it comes from earlier project research. **[not researched]** means no source was consulted — the topic is open.

## 1. Registration and formalities

**[not researched]** for Germany.

Establish before proposing any administrative action: the founder's legal form; whether their activity is a `Gewerbe` or a `freier Beruf`, because the registration route differs; whether they have registered with the tax office and received a `Steuernummer`; whether they hold a VAT identification number; and whether their activity falls under the `Handwerksordnung` with its own entry requirements.

Likely responsible bodies to check directly: the local `Gewerbeamt` for a `Gewerbeanmeldung`, the `Finanzamt` for tax registration, the `IHK` or `HWK` for chamber membership, and `ELSTER` for the tax questionnaire. **Do not state the steps, the fees or the deadlines from this pack** — none of them were verified.

## 2. VAT — Kleinunternehmerregelung (§19 UStG)

Two thresholds apply **together**:

| Condition | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total turnover in the **previous** calendar year must not have exceeded | **€25,000** |
| Total turnover in the **current** calendar year must not exceed | **€100,000** |

Source: [§19 UStG, official consolidated law text](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__19.html) · *primary legislation* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

The statute reads: *"steuerfrei, wenn der Gesamtumsatz nach Absatz 2 im vorangegangenen Kalenderjahr 25 000 Euro nicht überschritten hat und im laufenden Kalenderjahr 100 000 Euro nicht überschreitet."*

Both conditions must hold. Failing either one ends the exemption — this is a frequent misreading, since the €100,000 figure is often quoted alone.

Turnover from the sale of fixed assets is excluded from the total turnover calculation. The regime applies to entrepreneurs resident in Germany or in the territories designated in §1(3) UStG.

Reported changes since 2025, **not confirmed against the primary text**: the amounts are net rather than gross; exceeding the €100,000 limit ends the exemption immediately from the transaction that crosses it, rather than at year end; and the former pro-rata projection for a founding year no longer applies. · *secondary commentary* · **[checked 2026-11-10, needs primary confirmation]**

Effect: no VAT on invoices, and **no input VAT deduction** in return.

Recheck triggers: turnover approaching either threshold; the start of a new calendar year; the founding year specifically, where the projection rule matters.

## 3. B2B e-invoicing — receiving already mandatory, issuing phased to 2028

Source: [European Commission — eInvoicing in Germany](https://ec.europa.eu/digital-building-blocks/sites/spaces/DIGITAL/pages/467108886/eInvoicing+in+Germany) · *official EU source* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

| Date | Obligation |
| --- | --- |
| **2025-01-01** | Every business must be **able to receive** EN 16931-compliant structured e-invoices for domestic B2B |
| **2027-01-01** | Businesses with 2026 turnover above **€800,000** must **issue** structured e-invoices; paper invoices become prohibited for them |
| **2028-01-01** | The issuing obligation applies to **all** remaining businesses |

**The receiving obligation is already in force and it includes Kleinunternehmer.** Every business registered for German VAT, including those exempt under §19 UStG, must be able to receive a structured e-invoice. A founder who cannot accept one is already non-compliant on that point. · *EC page plus secondary commentary* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

Practical relief worth knowing: the EC page states that **simply providing an email address is sufficient** to meet the reception requirement. Receiving does not require buying a platform. Do not push a founder toward a paid product to satisfy an obligation an email address already satisfies. · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

Accepted formats: **XRechnung** (the national standard), **ZUGFeRD 2.2.0** (XRechnung profile), and **Peppol BIS Billing 3.0** — all conforming to EN 16931. · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

Legal basis: the `Wachstumschancengesetz` (Growth Opportunities Act), adopted **2024-03-22**, codified in **§14(2) and §27(38) UStG**. The Federal Ministry of Finance has published implementation guidance. · **[checked 2026-11-10]** — the BMF guidance itself is **[not researched]**.

For a solo founder the practical reading: **receive now, issue from 2028-01-01** unless turnover exceeds €800,000 in 2026, which the default scope of this pack makes unlikely. Confirm the issuing date against the founder's own turnover rather than assuming the later phase.

Recheck triggers: 2026 turnover approaching €800,000; before 2027-01-01 and again before 2028-01-01; publication of further BMF guidance.

## 6. Data protection — roles, suppliers and security

The GDPR applies directly. Germany additionally has the `BDSG` and **separate supervisory authorities per federal state**, so the competent authority depends on where the founder is established.

- Roles: [EDPB — data controller or data processor](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/learn-the-basics/data-controller-or-data-processor_en) · *EU regulator guidance* · **[carried 2026-10-27, not refetched]**
- Security: [EDPB — secure personal data](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/secure-personal-data_en) · *EU regulator guidance* · **[carried 2026-10-27, not refetched]**

Roles follow the actual purposes and instructions, not a product name or a contract title. Measures must be proportionate to the actual risk and context.

The BDSG and the relevant state authority's own guidance are **[not researched]**. EU AI Act applicability is not covered.
