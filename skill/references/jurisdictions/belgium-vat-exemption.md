# Belgium — the VAT small-business exemption (annex)

**This is an annex to [belgium.md](belgium.md) §2, not a pack of its own.** It exists because the Belgian pack reached this skill's 48 KB per-reference budget. §2 of the pack holds what a founder asks for — the figure in force, the enacted ladder to 2031, the 10 % tolerance on crossing mid-year, and the fact that being exempt does not exempt them from e-invoicing. The statutory machinery is here: who is excluded from the regime altogether, what counts toward the threshold, what entering the regime costs, what paperwork survives it, and how the EU cross-border version works. Same split as [belgium-e-invoicing.md](belgium-e-invoicing.md) and [germany-e-invoicing.md](germany-e-invoicing.md), for the same reason.

**This annex is preparation, not advice.** It records what the VAT Code and its royal decrees state, with dates. It is not a tax determination for any founder, and a consequential step needs a Belgian accountant or the FOD itself.

## 1. Who is shut out of the regime, whatever their turnover — art. 56bis

**The threshold is the second question. This is the first.** Art. 56bis §2 and §3 exclude people and activities from the exemption outright, and a founder who only ever checks the €25,000 figure will not know they are outside it.

**Art. 56bis §1 defines the three terms the rest of the subsection runs on**, and they are not interchangeable:

| Term | What the statute says |
| --- | --- |
| `jaaromzet in België` | the total annual amount, **VAT excluded**, of supplies of goods and services carried out **in Belgium** during a calendar year |
| `jaaromzet in de Unie` | the same total for supplies carried out **on the territory of the Community** |
| `gevestigde belastingplichtige` | a taxable person who has the **seat of their economic activity** in a member state, *"ongeacht of hij al dan niet beschikt over een vaste inrichting in een andere lidstaat"* — whether or not they have a fixed establishment in another member state |

**Excluded for the whole of their economic activity** — art. 56bis §2, second paragraph, *"voor het geheel van hun economische activiteit"* — are taxable persons who **regularly** (`geregeld`) carry out any of:

1. **`werk in onroerende staat`** within art. 19 §2 third paragraph, and the operations treated as equivalent to it. Immovable work: building, fitting out, repairing.
2. supplies for which they must give the customer the **cash-register ticket** of the royal decree of 30 December 2009 on registered cash systems in the horeca sector.
3. supplies of **old and used materials that cannot be reused as such, industrial and non-industrial waste, waste for reuse, partly processed waste and scrap** within art. 199(1)(d) of Directive 2006/112/EC. The King draws up the list.

**One carve-out from that list, and it is the one a solo founder might stand in**: the exclusion in 1° does not apply to services supplied by **taxable natural persons** under the conditions of art. 50 §4. So the construction exclusion is not automatic for every sole trader doing immovable work, and art. 50 §4 is the condition to check. · art. 50 §4 itself is **[not researched]**

**Art. 56bis §2 first paragraph** excludes **`btw-eenheden`** — VAT groups within art. 4 §2 — from the exemption.

**And art. 56bis §3 excludes five kinds of operation** from the regime even for someone otherwise inside it:

1. the operations referred to in art. 8 (certain supplies of buildings);
2. the supply of **new means of transport** within art. 8bis §2 first paragraph 2°, under the conditions of art. 39bis first paragraph 1° and 2°;
3. the operations in art. 58 §§1 and 2 (the special regimes — tobacco among them);
4. **operations carried out in a hidden way** — *"de handelingen die niet worden aangegeven en de handelingen die niet geoorloofd zijn"*, the undeclared and the unlawful;
5. the operations in art. 44 §3 2° a) third dash, except where carried out by a taxable natural person under the conditions of art. 50 §4.

**4° is worth saying plainly to a founder.** Undeclared turnover does not sit outside the threshold calculation as a quiet benefit; the statute puts those operations outside the exemption, so the exemption cannot be used to cover them.

## 2. What counts toward the threshold — art. 56nonies

**This is the article that decides whether a founder is near €25,000**, and three of its four limbs are not what a reasonable person would guess. The turnover serving as the measure consists of the following amounts, **VAT not included**:

1. supplies of goods and services **in so far as they would be taxed** if carried out by a taxable person outside the exemption;
2. operations taxed at the **zero rate** under art. 37, and where applicable operations exempt **with a right to deduct input VAT** under art. 98(2) or art. 105bis of Directive 2006/112/EC;
3. operations **exempt under arts. 39 to 42** — exports and intra-Community supplies among them — and where applicable those exempt under arts. 138, 146 to 149, 151, 152 and 153 of the Directive;
4. operations in **immovable property** under art. 44 §3 1° and 2°, **insurance and reinsurance** under art. 44 §3 4°, and **financial operations** under art. 44 §3 5° to 11° — with the Directive equivalents — **unless those operations are ancillary to other operations** (*"tenzij die handelingen met andere handelingen samenhangende handelingen zijn"*).

**So exempt cross-border sales still count.** Limb 3 pulls exports and intra-Community supplies into the measure. A founder who invoices a Dutch or German client without Belgian VAT has not thereby kept that turnover out of the €25,000.

**And one thing that does not count**, in the article's closing paragraph: *"De overdracht van materiële of immateriële investeringsgoederen van de belastingplichtige wordt niet in aanmerking genomen"* — **transferring the business's own capital goods, tangible or intangible, is left out of the calculation.** Selling the laptop, the van or a piece of software the business itself used does not push anyone over the threshold.

**Art. 56sexies ties the reporting to this article.** For the cross-border provisions, the amounts to be declared are *"de in artikel 56nonies bedoelde bedragen"*, expressed **in euro** — and where a member state granting the exemption operates **several thresholds** under art. 284(1) second paragraph of the Directive, the taxable person reports the total separately for each threshold that could apply. The data goes to *"het daarvoor door de minister van Financiën of zijn gemachtigde gecreëerde elektronische adres"*.

## 3. The cross-border version, and how it is switched off — arts. 56septies and 56octies

**Art. 56septies is the relief**, and it runs in both directions:

- **§1** — a taxable person established in **another member state** who uses the Belgian exemption does not have to be VAT-identified under art. 50 §1 first paragraph 1°, and files **no** return under art. 53 §1 first paragraph 2°, for the supplies the regime covers. *"Het eerste lid is niet van toepassing indien de belastingplichtige de in artikel 284 ter van Richtlijn 2006/112/EG bedoelde verplichtingen niet nakomt"* — **the relief lapses if they do not meet the Directive's art. 284ter obligations.**
- **§2** — a **Belgian-established** taxable person using the exemption in another member state where they are not established does not have to be identified under arts. 213 and 214 of the Directive, and files no art. 250 return there. Same proviso, against art. 56quinquies.

**So the relief is conditional on the reporting.** It is not a regime a founder can enter and then go quiet in: miss the quarterly figures and the identification and return duties come back.

**Art. 56octies lists the four events that deactivate the `EX` number** — or, where the person keeps using the exemption in other member states, adjust the data held for the member states concerned, in each case *"zonder verwijl"*:

1. the total declared supplies **exceed the amount in art. 284(2)(a) of the Directive** — the Union cap;
2. the other member state granting the exemption **reports that the person no longer qualifies** there, or that the exemption no longer applies;
3. the person **reports their own decision to stop** using the regime;
4. the person reports, **or the administration can otherwise assume**, that their activities have ended.

**Art. 56undecies** leaves the modalities to the King: the form of the prior notification under arts. 56ter §1 fourth paragraph and 56quater §1 1°, changes and cessation of activity or of tax regime, **the way revisions are carried out when the tax regime changes**, and how the art. 56quater §3 and 56quinquies data reaches the administration. That last-but-one item is the delegation behind `KB nr. 19` art. 4 in §5 below — the cost of entering the regime.

### What art. 56ter actually says, and the two counting rules a founder will not guess

Art. 56ter §1 gives the exemption to taxable persons established in Belgium whose **annual turnover in Belgium is not more than €25,000**, for the supplies they make in Belgium. Three further sentences matter more than the figure:

- **Joint activity is added together.** Where several people carry on an economic activity *"in onverdeeldheid of in vereniging"*, the threshold is applied to *"het jaarlijks totaalbedrag van de omzetcijfers die ze samen realiseren"* — their combined turnover.
- **Spouses are counted separately.** Where spouses carry on **distinct** economic activities, each activity is counted on its own, *"ongeacht hun huwelijksvoorwaarden"* — whatever their matrimonial property regime.
- **The notification is made at the start.** A taxable person wanting the exemption notifies the administration *"op het moment van de aanvang van zijn economische activiteit"*. And where the circumstances clearly show the conditions are not met, the administration puts them under another regime instead.

**Art. 56ter §2 is the inbound half of the EU scheme**: a taxable person established in another member state may use the Belgian exemption where their **Union annual turnover is at most €100,000** — at the ECB rate published on **2018-01-18** — **and** their Belgian turnover is at most €25,000, having first notified their own member state and been identified there with the **`EX`** suffix. That is Belgium's side of the mechanism [eu-vat-sme.md](eu-vat-sme.md) reads from the directive.

**Art. 56quater is the outbound half.** A Belgian-established taxable person wanting another member state's exemption sends a prior notification *"aan het daarvoor door de minister van Financiën of zijn gemachtigde gecreëerde elektronisch adres"*, and is identified by their Belgian VAT number **with `EX` appended**. The notification carries the name, activity, legal form and address, the member states concerned, and the totals supplied in Belgium and in each other member state **in the preceding calendar year and in the elapsed part of the current one**. The exemption takes effect in the other state from the date the administration communicates the identification number.

**Two deadlines come with it**: the quarterly figures are supplied **within one month** of the end of the calendar quarter, and where the Union threshold is crossed the administration must be told **within fifteen working days**, together with the amount supplied from the start of the current quarter to the date of crossing.

### Getting into the exemption costs something — `KB nr. 19` arts. 3 and 4

**Starting out is simple.** Art. 3 §1: a taxable person beginning an activity notifies the intention in advance through the start-of-activity declaration under `KB nr. 10`; it has effect **for an indefinite period from the first day of activity**; and the VAT identification number is allotted **within fifteen working days**, unless that is too short for the checks needed to prevent evasion.

**Switching in later is a calendar exercise.** Art. 3 §2: a taxable person under the normal or flat-rate regime whose current-year turnover stays under the threshold may move to the exemption from **1 January, 1 April, 1 July or 1 October**, by applying **before 15 December** of the current year or **before 15 March, 15 June or 15 September**. The application states the turnover realised in the elapsed quarters **and an estimate for the remaining ones**.

**And art. 4 is the cost nobody budgets for.** On moving into the exemption the taxable person must **revise the VAT already deducted** on goods and services other than capital assets not yet used or consumed, and on capital assets still in existence and usable whose original deduction is open to revision. The transition *"wordt gelijkgesteld met het verlies van de hoedanigheid van belastingplichtige"* — it is treated as **losing taxable-person status** — and the amount to repay is fixed on the basis of an **inventory** of what is left. A founder who has just bought equipment and then switches into the exemption pays some of that deduction back.

### What the exemption does to the paperwork — `KB nr. 1` art. 14

Source: [`KB nr. 1`, art. 14 §§2, 4 and 5 and arts. 17 and 18](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/eli/besluit/1992/12/29/1992003823/justel) · *primary legislation, Justel consolidated text; §4 as replaced by `KB 2024-12-15/05`, in force 2025-01-01* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**

**Art. 14 §4**: a taxable person under **art. 56ter** is relieved of keeping the incoming- and outgoing-invoice books that §2 1° and 2° otherwise require — **on two conditions**: the invoices and documents are kept *"volgens een ononderbroken reeks volgnummers"* assigned on receipt, issue or drawing up, **and** the book in §5 is kept. So the exemption removes the ledgers and replaces them with a numbered file plus one book; it does not remove the obligation to keep anything.

**Two figures from the same decree that a founder will meet either way.** Art. 17 §1: the periodic return is filed by the **twentieth** day of the month following the period. Art. 17 §2: a **quarterly** return is allowed, by the **twenty-fifth** day of the month after each calendar quarter, where annual turnover excluding VAT is **at most €2,500,000** — with a lower €250,000 limit for energy products, mobile phones and computers with their accessories, and registered motor vehicles. Art. 18 §1 requires returns to be filed **electronically**, and §2 relieves that duty *"zolang zij niet over de nodige geïnformatiseerde middelen beschikken"*.
