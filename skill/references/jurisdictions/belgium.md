# Belgium — jurisdiction pack

Scope: a solo founder (natural person or single-member company) established in Belgium, selling a digital service to Belgian business customers, no employees, small bounded pilot.

Out of scope and requiring separate research: consumer (B2C) sales, employees or subcontractors, regulated professions, cross-border supply, physical goods, payment or financial services, health data.

**This pack is preparation, not advice.** It records what official sources state, with dates. It is not a tax, legal or compliance determination for any founder. Consequential steps need a Belgian accountant, business counter (`ondernemingsloket` / `guichet d'entreprises`) or lawyer.

Verification convention: **[checked YYYY-MM-DD]** means the source was actually fetched and read on that date. **[carried YYYY-MM-DD, not refetched]** means earlier research was reused without refetching it. **[not researched]** means no source was consulted — the topic is open.

## 1. Registration and formalities

Source: [FPS Economy — procedures for the self-employed](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/enterprises/starting-business/procedures-self-employed) · *official* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

The formalities it names, with the responsible body:

| Formality | Body | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Register as a company in the **Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (CBE/KBO/BCE)** | CBE, via an approved business counter | Gives the enterprise number used as the identification key |
| **Join a social insurance fund** for the self-employed | A fund of the founder's own choosing | The page states this must be done **prior to starting the activity** |
| Check whether you are **subject to VAT** and activate if so | General Tax Administration, FPS Finance | The enterprise number becomes the VAT number; free when requested directly |
| Identify as an **employer** with the NSSO, plus Dimona declaration | NSSO | Only if hiring staff — outside this pack's scope |

The page gives **no specific deadlines** beyond "prior to starting your activity" for the social insurance fund, and does **not** list health insurance or a bank account as mandatory formalities.

Establish before advising any administrative action: whether the founder is already in the CBE and under which activity codes; their VAT situation (identified, under the small-business exemption, or not identified); their social-insurance affiliation; and their region of establishment.

| Source | What it says | Checked |

Specific professional competences for certain regulated trades are **maintained** — it names restaurateurs, bakers and pastry chefs, roofers and heating engineers. If so, you must also be able to demonstrate basic knowledge of business management. This is not required in Flanders or Brussels."* No content update date shown. | 2026-11-10 |
| [FPS Economy procedures](https://economie.fgov.be/en/themes/enterprises/starting-business/procedures-self-employed) | States competence requirements apply in **Wallonia and the Brussels-Capital Region** — which contradicts the other two on Brussels. | 2026-11-10 |

Consequence for the founder: **do not create a task to obtain the abolished proof**, and do not rely on any of the three for a Brussels founder. The regional announcement is the most specific and most recent, but two federal portals still contradict it and each other. Confirm with the founder's own region before acting, and still check whether their **actual activity** is a regulated trade with a retained competence requirement.

Recheck trigger: a change of region; the activity turning out to be a regulated trade; either federal portal being corrected.

## 2. VAT — small-business exemption regime

Current threshold **in force**: **€25,000** annual Belgian turnover, excluding VAT. This is the figure to use today.

### The phased increase: the figures are confirmed, the enactment is not

Source: [Chamber of Representatives, **DOC 56 1593/001** of **2026-06-15**](https://www.dekamer.be/FLWB/PDF/56/1593/56K1593001.pdf), `Wetsontwerp op de reductie van de kosten II`, Title 5 · *official parliamentary document, PDF extracted and the enacting articles read in both languages* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

**Article 25** of that bill replaces the first paragraph of art. 56ter §1 of the VAT Code, and the 2° of art. 56ter §2 first paragraph, with these amounts:

| Year | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| **2026** | **€25,000** — in force now, unchanged by this bill |
| 2027 | €26,000 |
| 2028 | €27,000 |
| 2029 | €28,000 |
| 2030 | €29,000 |
| 2031 and following | €30,000 |

**Article 26** of the bill is not an amending provision — it is the entry into force: *"Het artikel 25 treedt in werking op 1 januari 2027."*

**Status, stated exactly.** This is a **bill**, and the amounts above are its proposed text:

- Tabled in the Chamber on **2026-06-15** as DOC 56 1593/001; the Council of State raised no objection. · *bill read directly*
- **Approved by the Chamber on 2026-09-17.** · *secondary — reported by [vereniginginfo.be](https://www.vereniginginfo.be/blog/vzws/btw-vrijstellingsregeling-kleine-ondernemingen-van-25000-naar-30000-euro/302/) on 2026-09-25, which calls it a `wetsontwerp goedgekeurd`*
- **The amending law exists. Its contents were not readable.** On 2026-11-11 the Justel amendment history for the VAT Code was read directly, and it lists *"Wet van 23-09-2026 gepubliceerd op 07-10-2026 — Gewijzigd artikel: 56ter"*. So a law of **2026-09-23, published 2026-10-07, did amend art. 56ter** — which is the article the bill above was to replace. What that law changed art. 56ter **to** is a separate question, and Justel did not serve the consolidated text of the Code to an automated reader on that date. **So still do not tell a founder these amounts are law** — but do name that instrument, because it is what a Belgian adviser would look up in a minute. Chamber approval is the decisive parliamentary step, so enactment is likely — but royal assent and publication are what make it law, and neither has been seen here.

**Correction recorded, and now partly un-corrected — which is worth more than either state alone.** An earlier version of this entry stated the basis as *"now enacted, not a bill: the law of 2026-09-23, published 2026-10-07, article 26 amends article 56ter"*, and it was withdrawn on 2026-11-10 as unverified. Reading Justel's amendment history on 2026-11-11 establishes that **the instrument it named is real, with exactly that date and publication date, and it does amend art. 56ter**. The withdrawal was still right at the time: the claim rested on nothing the pack had read. What was wrong was not the fact but the evidence, and the fix was to find the evidence rather than to restate the claim. Reading the primary document on 2026-11-10 showed three errors: the amending article is **25**, not 26 — 26 is the entry-into-force clause; the instrument is a **bill of 2026-06-15**, not a law of 2026-09-23; and neither of the two secondary sources then cited ever claimed publication. One of them is dated 2026-06-26 and says only *"In de Kamer is een wetsontwerp ingediend"*. **The pack asserted enactment that its own sources did not support** — the same class of error as the French PLF 2026 entry, in the opposite direction.

Both earlier instruments are confirmed in the same amendment history, with publication dates the pack did not have: the **law of 2024-03-21, published 2024-04-09**, amended arts. 56, 56bis and 56ter among a long list, and the **law of 2026-02-10, published 2026-02-20**, amended arts. 56bis and 56ter. Those are the instruments behind the €25,000 figure that *is* in force. The history also shows the Code amended three more times in 2026 alone, most recently by a law of **2026-10-25, published 2026-10-30**, which did not touch the small-business articles — a reason to check the history itself on any recheck rather than trusting a figure.

Headlines reading "threshold raised to €30,000" are misleading twice over: €30,000 applies only from **2031**, and only if the bill is enacted.

What the exemption does: supply without charging VAT, relieved of periodic VAT returns, while keeping the VAT identification. The trade-off is **loss of input VAT deduction** on purchases and investments relating to the exempt activity. This describes the regime art. 56ter of the VAT Code establishes; it is not taken from a source read for this pack, and the Code itself has not been read here.

Separate EU cross-border scheme: the €100,000 Union annual turnover cap comes from art. 284(2)(a) of Council Directive (EU) 2020/285 and applies from 2025-01-01, alongside the national threshold. Read and quoted from EUR-Lex in [eu-vat-sme.md](eu-vat-sme.md), which is where that claim and its check date are maintained.

Recheck triggers: turnover approaching the threshold; the start of any calendar year; any cross-border customer.

## 3. B2B e-invoicing — in force now

**Already in effect.** Applies to invoices *created, issued or sent* from **2026-01-01** (`opgemaakt, uitgereikt of verzonden vanaf 1 januari 2026`).

Source: [official e-invoicing FAQ — general B2B questions](https://efactuur.belgium.be/nl/FAQ/algemene-vragen-b2b) · *the administration's own FAQ, not the statute* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

| Situation | Position |
| --- | --- |
| Belgian VAT-taxable business invoicing another Belgian VAT-taxable business (B2B) | **In scope — must issue structured e-invoices** |
| Business using the small-business VAT exemption regime (turnover ≤ €25,000) | **In scope — the obligation applies** |
| Special agricultural flat-rate regime | In scope **at least for receiving** B2B invoices (`minstens voor het ontvangen van B2B-facturen`) |
| VAT taxpayer not established in Belgium, without a fixed establishment, even if VAT-identified here | **Out of scope** — the FAQ notes the law will be adjusted on this point |
| Selling only to consumers (B2C) | Not mandatory |
| A person with only article 44 VAT-exempt activities | **Unknown** — the FAQ does not address this case, and no other source was consulted for it: **[not researched]**. Do not infer either way. |

Format and transport: a structured electronic invoice over the **Peppol** network. Legal hook: new article 53, §2bis of the Belgian VAT Code.

**Why this matters most for this pack's default user.** A Belgian solo founder running a small B2B pilot is very likely under the €25,000 exemption regime — and that regime does **not** remove this obligation. A PDF emailed to a Belgian business customer is not a structured electronic invoice. Do not treat a PDF-only workflow as sufficient by default.

Equally: do not push the founder toward a paid invoicing vendor without an established requirement and their own authority to spend. Establish the actual parties, place of establishment, VAT status and transaction type, then confirm the route with an accountant.

Recheck triggers: the announced legal adjustment for non-established taxpayers; any change in VAT status; a non-Belgian or non-business customer; before the founder issues their first real invoice.

## 4. Agreements and unfair terms

Source for this whole section: the FPS Economy brochure [*Gedaan met de wet van de sterkste, deel 4*](https://economie.fgov.be/sites/default/files/Files/Publications/files/gedaan-met-de-wet-van-de-sterkste-deel-4.pdf) · *official explainer, text extracted and read* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**. Its embedded metadata dates it 2020-09 authored, 2021-04 modified, so it predates later case law. The provision numbers and examples below are the brochure's; the WER text itself has not been read for this section.

**B2B does not mean unrestricted contracting.** Belgian law applies unfair-terms control between businesses, in three layers.

| Layer | Provision | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General norm** | art. VI.91/3 §1 WER | A term creating a *manifest imbalance* (`kennelijk onevenwicht`) between the parties' rights and obligations — alone or combined with other terms — is unfair |
| **Black list** | art. VI.91/4 WER | Always unfair, irrebuttably. No counter-evidence possible. |
| **Grey list** | art. VI.91/5 WER | Presumed unfair, but the presumption is rebuttable |

The regime sits in art. VI.91/1–VI.91/10 WER, introduced by the law of **4 April 2019**, with the unfair-terms provisions applying from **2020-12-01**. Its stated aim is protecting smaller businesses against counterparties they are economically dependent on — which is the founder's likely position in a pilot.

### Scope — four limits the brochure states

- Applies to **all businesses regardless of legal form**.
- Only to agreements **concluded after 2020-12-01**, the entry into force. An older contract is outside it.
- **Does not apply to financial services or public procurement.**
- Covers **every clause** — both standard terms and ones you individually negotiated. And it does **not matter** whether a party is strong or dependent: *"waar het om gaat is de beoordeling of het betrokken beding al dan niet onrechtmatig is."*

### The subject-matter exclusion is conditional on transparency

Clauses about the **subject matter itself** — the service, the goods, the agreed price — are outside the review, **provided they are formulated transparently**. Where they are not clear and comprehensible, they **can** be reviewed.

The brochure's own examples of what that catches: extra costs buried in the small print and not included in the quoted price, or a service that normally forms part of the offering but is excluded in the fine print.

Everything else — price adjustment, termination, penalties, jurisdiction, exclusion or limitation of liability — is tested on **two** aspects: **transparency** (can the other business see and understand the economic and legal consequences?) and **balance** (does it create too great an imbalance against the party agreeing to it?). The assessment weighs the circumstances of conclusion, the nature of the products, trade usages, and the other clauses of this or any related agreement.

### Black list — art. VI.91/4 WER: four clauses, always unfair, no counter-evidence

With the brochure's own examples, which are more useful than the abstractions:

| | Clause | The brochure's example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | You are bound while the **counterparty can make its own commitment depend on its own will** | You place an order; the supplier's terms say its management must confirm the request before it becomes final |
| 2 | Only the counterparty **reserves the right to interpret** a clause | A telecom operator may demand a guarantee for consumption it finds "abnormally high", with no threshold in the contract, so it decides what is abnormal |
| 3 | **Excluding or limiting access to a court** — waiving legal action in a dispute | A clause forbidding you from joining a collective redress action |
| 4 | Establishing **knowledge or acceptance** of other clauses you could not actually read before concluding | "You accept the general terms", where those terms were never communicated before the contract was concluded |

### Grey list — art. VI.91/5 WER: eight clauses, presumed unfair, rebuttable

| | Clause | The brochure's example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Unilateral change** of price, terms or characteristics, unless the business shows valid reasons | Changing the price of an ongoing service with no justification |
| 2 | **Automatic renewal or extension** without specifying a reasonable notice period | A one-year supply contract that renews with no window to opt out beforehand |
| 3 | Placing **economic risk** on a party who in principle should not bear it, **without economic consideration** | A supplier obliged to take back unsold goods with no compensation; a seller bearing theft risk after delivery with nothing in return |
| 4 | **Excluding or limiting the other party's rights** on non-performance or defective performance, without valid reason | **Delivering technological services *"zoals ze zijn"* or *"volgens beschikbaarheid"* — "as is" or "as available" — limiting liability when it does not work** |
| 5 | The **absence of a reasonable notice period**. Reasonableness is sector-dependent; the right not to be bound for life and to change business partner is treated as essential, balanced against giving the other side time to adapt and find a replacement | — |
| 6 | **Exempting the business from liability** for fraud, serious fault, that of its employees, or failure to perform its principal services | An architect limiting his statutory supervisory duty to a periodic site visit |
| 7 | **Restricting the means of evidence** the other party may use | Only documents the business itself issues may be used against it; or an app it provides to customers may never be used as evidence against it |
| 8 | **Excessive damages clauses** for non-performance or delay — where the stipulated sum is clearly above a reasonable estimate of the harm | A flat 10% of the unpaid amount, **cumulated** with €20 per reminder **and** default interest. The flat sum is deemed to cover all extrajudicial recovery costs, reminders included |

**Third category — the general norm.** Beyond both lists, a clause can be unfair simply because, in the circumstances, it creates a manifest imbalance between the parties' rights and obligations.

### What a founder should take from this

**Grey item 4 is the one that hits a software founder directly.** An *"as is"* or *"as available"* clause for a technological service is on the grey list by name. Almost every SaaS and software agreement has one. It is not void, but it is **presumed unfair** — so a founder relying on it must be able to justify it, and a founder *facing* one has a presumption on their side.

Black items 3 and 4 catch ordinary drafting: "the client waives any claim" is void outright, and so is "the client is deemed to have accepted these terms" where they never had a real chance to read them — which is also independently a ground under Dutch art. 6:233 — see [netherlands.md](netherlands.md).

Grey item 8 is worth reading before drafting a late-payment clause: the example condemns exactly the common stack of a percentage **plus** per-reminder fees **plus** interest, and says the flat sum is taken to cover the reminders.

**Still open:** this is the official explainer, not the statute. Before relying on a specific item, read art. VI.91/4 and VI.91/5 in the consolidated Code of Economic Law, and check for case law since 2021 — the brochure predates it. · **[not researched]**

For the founder's own discussion sheet, cover: parties, the specific outcome promised, required inputs, exclusions, delivery window, how acceptance is demonstrated, price and payment, duration, exit, what happens on failure, support, and responsibility for data and rights. These are proposed agreement topics, not a legally sufficient contract.

Never draft a blanket liability waiver, a unilateral acceptance clause, an automatic renewal, or an invented service guarantee — given a black and grey list exist, such clauses are exactly where the risk sits. Leave enforceability for a Belgian legal reviewer and preserve any disagreement with the exact clauses concerned.

Recheck trigger: before any contract is signed; publication of a readable official explainer.

## 5. Ownership and permitted use

Source: [FPS Economy — who owns the copyright on software](https://economie.fgov.be/nl/themas/intellectuele-eigendom/intellectuele-eigendomsrechten/auteursrecht-en-naburige/auteursrechten/intellectuele-eigendom-op/creatie-van-een/wie-bezit-de-auteursrechten-op) · *official* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

Three distinct rules:

| Situation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Independent author** (the default) | The author or authors own the rights, unless they transferred them: *"De auteur of auteurs van een computerprogramma bezitten de rechten hierop, tenzij ze die rechten hebben overgedragen aan derden."* |
| **Created by an employee** under an employment contract or statutory position | The **employer automatically acquires the economic rights**: *"verkrijgt de werkgever de vermogensrechten op het computerprogramma, behoudens andersluidende overeenkomst."* The employee cannot use or dispose of it without permission, absent a different agreement. |
| **Commissioned software** | Rights are **not** automatically transferred to the client: *"worden de rechten op het computerprogramma niet automatisch overgedragen aan de klant."* By default only **usage rights** pass to the client while the author retains ownership. The parties may agree to transfer all economic rights, but that transfer must be **explicit and in writing**. |

Practical consequence for a pilot: a founder delivering commissioned software keeps ownership by default, and a client who wants the rights needs an explicit written transfer. A founder who *bought* work from a freelancer does **not** own it merely by having paid.

### The statute behind that guidance, and what it makes unwaivable

Source: [Wetboek van economisch recht, Boek XI, Titel 6 — computerprogramma's](https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/change_lg.pl?language=nl&la=N&table_name=wet&cn=2014041960) · *primary legislation, articles read individually* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

**Art. XI.296 confirms the employment rule, and confirms its limit:**

> *"Tenzij bij overeenkomst of statutair anders is bepaald, wordt alleen de werkgever geacht verkrijger te zijn van de vermogensrechten met betrekking tot computerprogramma's die zijn gemaakt door een of meer **werknemers of beambten** bij de uitoefening van hun taken of in opdracht van hun werkgever."*

Note what the words do: the `opdracht` in that sentence is an instruction **from their own employer** to an employee or official. It is not a commission to an independent contractor, so XI.296 does not reach one. The guidance above is now backed by the text rather than only by the authority's summary.

**Art. XI.299 to XI.301 set out what the lawful user may do** — and XI.301 states plainly which of it a contract cannot touch: *"Het bepaalde in de artikelen XI.299, §§ 2 en 3, en XI.300 is van dwingend recht."*

| | What the lawful user may do | Can a contract remove it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| XI.299 §1 | Acts necessary to use the program **for its intended purpose, including correcting errors** | **Yes** — the paragraph opens *"Tenzij bij overeenkomst uitdrukkelijk anders is bepaald"* |
| XI.299 §2 | A **reservekopie** where necessary to use the program | **No** — `dwingend recht` |
| XI.299 §3 | **Observe, study and test** the program's operation to determine the underlying ideas and principles, while lawfully loading, displaying, running, transmitting or storing it | **No** — `dwingend recht` |
| XI.300 | **Reproduce and translate the code** where indispensable for interoperability of an independently created program, under three conditions, and with three bans on what the information may then be used for | **No** — `dwingend recht` |

**This is the same structure as France's, from the same Directive — see the comparison in [README.md](README.md).** For a founder the reading is practical: you cannot contract a client out of their backup copy, their right to study the program, or their right to decompile it for interoperability. You *can* reserve error correction, and that is the term to think about deliberately rather than by accident.

One more allocation worth knowing, from art. XI.298 c): the **first sale** in the EU of a copy of a program by or with the consent of the right holder **exhausts** the right to control distribution of that copy — except the right to control further **rental and lending**.

Inventory separately: original work, customer inputs, collaborator contributions, third-party components, images and data, and generated output. For each record provenance, the actual licence, required notices, and intended use and distribution.

Never infer ownership or clearance from payment alone, from the fact that AI generated something, or from a repository's top-level licence. Where rights are unresolved, resolve them with review or omit the material.

## 6. Data protection

**The GDPR layer is shared — read [eu-gdpr.md](eu-gdpr.md)** for the controller/processor test, the processor-contract duties, the sub-processor rule, the register obligation, the 72-hour breach notification and the security principle. Those rules are identical in all four packs and are not repeated here.

**Belgian specifics.** The supervisory authority is the **Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorité de protection des données (GBA/APD)**, and it publishes more SME-specific material than most — point a Belgian founder at these rather than paraphrasing them.

**[belgium-data-protection.md](belgium-data-protection.md) holds the Belgian layer:** the GBA's register guidance and its SME publications with the warning about two of them, the two-part breach portal and the account you need before the clock starts, **the `wet van 30 juli 2018` read — including art. 222, under which processing without a lawful basis is a criminal offence carrying up to €120,000** — and **the GBA's eight-entry art. 35(4) list, whose second entry has no scale qualifier at all**.
