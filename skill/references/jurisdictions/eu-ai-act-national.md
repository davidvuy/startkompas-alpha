# EU — AI Act, the national layer (annex)

**This is an annex to [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §7, not a pack of its own.** The Regulation's obligations are uniform; what each member state does under **art. 70** (who supervises) and **art. 99(1)** (what a breach costs nationally) is not. §7 of the layer says so and sends you here.

**The finding is the spread, not a table of fines.** Every member state had to designate at least one notifying authority and one market surveillance authority **by 2025-08-02**. Fifteen months later the three states read here are in three different conditions:

| | Who supervises | National penalties | State as at 2026-11-12 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Germany** | the **Bundesnetzagentur**, by statute | the Union tiers, **plus German offences of its own** at up to €50,000 | **Settled.** `KI-MIG` in force 2026-07-29 |
| **Netherlands** | **proposed**: ten existing market surveillance authorities, with the AP as the fallback and the AP and RDI coordinating | the bill ties them to art. 99; nothing is in force | **A closed consultation.** Opened 2026-04-20, closed 2026-06-01, not enacted |
| **Belgium** | **not named by anyone** this annex could read | **[not researched]** — there is no instrument to read | *"De implementatie van het governancekader in België is momenteel lopende"* — FOD Economie, 2026-09-03 |
| **France** | **[not researched]** | **[not researched]** | Légifrance serves a bot challenge; see [france-data-protection.md](france-data-protection.md) |

**What a founder does with that.** In Germany there is an authority and a price. In the Netherlands there is a named future authority and no enforceable national penalty yet, so the Union tiers in [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §6 are the whole exposure. In Belgium there is nobody designated to go to — which is **not** the same as nothing applying: the Regulation's prohibitions and the art. 4 literacy duty bind directly, and the FOD Economie and the Commission's own service desk are where a Belgian founder's question goes in the meantime.

**This annex is preparation, not advice.** It records what each instrument and each authority states, with dates. A national regime that is a bill is written as a bill.

Source: [`KI-Marktüberwachungs-und-Innovationsförderungs-Gesetz` (KI-MIG) of 2026-07-22, BGBl. 2026 I Nr. 223](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ki-mig/BJNR0DF0B0026.html) · *primary legislation, in force 2026-07-29* · **[checked 2026-11-11]**

§6 above says the Union ceilings are here and the national regimes are not. One of them is now read, because a founder in the largest member state covered by these packs should not be told only that a national regime exists. **This is Germany's, and it is not a template for anyone else's.**

**Who supervises.** §2(1): *"Die Bundesnetzagentur ist die für die Einhaltung der Verordnung (EU) 2024/1689 zuständige Marktüberwachungsbehörde, soweit in diesem Gesetz nichts anderes bestimmt ist"* — the **Bundesnetzagentur**, unless the Act says otherwise. The exceptions are sectoral: authorities already supervising the Annex I Section A harmonisation legislation keep those systems under §2(2), and the Act carves out a long list of financial-sector entities for BaFin. §6: the Bundesnetzagentur is also the **single point of contact** under art. 70(2), and §8 establishes a central complaints office, for which the Länder may name single contact points. §13: the Bundesnetzagentur **must establish and run at least one AI regulatory sandbox** under arts. 57 and 58.

**This closes a question the German pack raised from the other side.** A Bavarian supervisory authority's checklist recorded in May 2025 that the authority competent for the AI Act in Germany was not yet settled — see [germany-data-protection.md](germany-data-protection.md). It was settled on 2026-07-29, fourteen months later, and the answer is the telecoms regulator rather than the data protection authorities.

**What Germany added to the fines, and it is small.** The Regulation's own ceilings in art. 99(3) to (5) are enforced here through §16 and §17, with the market surveillance authority acting as the administrative authority. Separately, **§15 creates German administrative offences of its own**, for intentional or negligent breaches of specific articles: art. 21(1) and (2) (information and access for authorities), art. 27(1), (2) and (3) (the fundamental-rights impact assessment), and art. 45(1) to (3) (informing notifying authorities). §15(2) adds one aimed squarely at a deployer:

> a deployer of a high-risk system under **Annex III points 1, 3, 4 or 5** who does not ensure that an affected person is given the explanation required by **art. 86(1)**

§15(3): *"Die Ordnungswidrigkeit kann mit einer Geldbuße bis zu fünfzigtausend Euro geahndet werden"* — **up to €50,000.**

Read that beside §3: Annex III points 4 and 5 are employment and essential services, which is where an HR screening tool and a credit-scoring tool sit. **So in Germany, failing to explain a high-risk decision to the person it was about is a named offence with its own ceiling**, separate from the Union tiers. The Act does not give the art. 86(1) duty a new content; it gives it a German enforcement price.

**One drafting detail worth recording rather than interpreting.** §15 pins its offences to the Regulation *"in der Fassung vom 13. Juni 2024"* — the version of 13 June 2024, which is the original. The amending Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in §0 applied from **2026-07-27**, two days before the KI-MIG entered into force. What that reference does to offences defined against articles the Omnibus has since changed is **[not researched]** and is a question for a German lawyer, not for this layer. It is recorded because a reader comparing the two dates will notice, and should know the pack noticed too.

## The Netherlands — a bill, not a law, and that is the answer

Sources
- [`Toezicht op AI wordt concreet: sleutelrol voor de AP en de RDI`](https://autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl/actueel/toezicht-op-ai-wordt-concreet-sleutelrol-voor-de-ap-en-de-rdi) · *official authority, 2026-04-20* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**
- [`Kabinet zet stap met toezicht op Europese AI-regels`](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/04/20/kabinet-zet-stap-met-toezicht-op-europese-ai-regels) · *official, the responsible ministry, 2026-04-20* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**
- [`Consultatie Uitvoeringswet AI-verordening`](https://www.internetconsultatie.nl/uaiv/b1) · *official consultation register — status `Consultatie gesloten`, reactions dated 2026-06-01* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**

**There is no Dutch AI Act supervision or penalty regime in force.** The `Uitvoeringswet AI-verordening` went into internet consultation on **2026-04-20** and closed on **2026-06-01**. The consultation register's own words for the supervisory system are *"het voorgenomen Nederlandse toezichtstelsel"* — the **intended** system. The AP's announcement ends the same way: *"De uitvoeringswet is nu nog in consultatie"*, with *"de verdere invulling volgt na de consultatie en parlementaire behandeling."*

**What the bill proposes**, and it is worth knowing because it tells a founder who will eventually call:

- A **hybrid model with ten market surveillance authorities**, each supervising AI inside its own domain — the AFM and DNB keep financial supervision, the inspectorates keep product safety — *"[d]aardoor krijgen organisaties zoveel mogelijk te maken met toezichthouders die zij al kennen."*
- **Where a field has no clear supervisor, the AP gets it**, with a dedicated AI board member.
- The **AP** is proposed as supervisor for **prohibited AI practices**, the **transparency obligations** — the AP's examples are *"herkenbaarheid van chatbots en deepfakes"* — and *"een groot deel van de hoog-risico AI-toepassingen, zoals in werk, onderwijs en overheid."*
- The **AP and the RDI** coordinate, and are to run an **AI regulatory sandbox** from 2026.

**The work row is the one that matters here.** A recruitment screening tool is high-risk under Annex III point 4(a) — see [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §3 — and the Dutch proposal puts *"werk"* with the AP. So the founder in eval case C24 would, once this is law, answer to the same authority that already supervises their GDPR obligations. **Today they answer to nobody nationally, and that is not relief**: the Regulation binds without the implementing act, as the AP itself says — *"belangrijke delen van de AI-verordening zijn al van kracht."*

**And one caution that the pack predicted.** The consultation page states that the Regulation's remaining parts apply *"in augustus 2026 respectievelijk augustus 2027"*. That is the **pre-Omnibus** schedule. [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §5 warns that "a summary written before July 2026 has this wrong in both directions", and here is an official government page from April 2026 doing exactly that. **Take the dates from art. 113 as amended, not from a national explanatory page.**

## Belgium — nobody is designated, and the department says so

Sources
- [`Veelgestelde vragen over artificiële intelligentie, de Artificial Intelligence Act en de toepassing daarvan`](https://economie.fgov.be/nl/themas/online/artificiele-intelligentie/veelgestelde-vragen-over) · *official authority, FOD Economie, page last updated 2026-09-03* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**
- [`AI Act` theme page for businesses](https://economie.fgov.be/nl/themas/ondernemingen/ai-act) · *official authority, FOD Economie, last updated 2026-09-10* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**
- [`Toepassing van de AI-verordening`](https://www.bipt.be/sectoren/digitaal/ai-verordening/toepassing-van-de-ai-verordening) · *official authority, BIPT — the phased application timeline* · **[checked 2026-11-12]**

**The FOD Economie's own FAQ states the duty and not the answer.** It sets out that *"[t]egen 2 augustus 2025 moet iedere lidstaat ten minste één aanmeldende autoriteit en één markttoezichtautoriteit aanstellen als nationale bevoegde autoriteiten"*, explains what each does — and then says:

> De implementatie van het governancekader in België is momenteel lopende.

On a page last updated **2026-09-03**: thirteen months after the deadline the same page quotes. **The FAQ names no Belgian authority at all** — not the BIPT, not the FOD, not a regional body. So a Belgian founder asking "who supervises this for me" has no answer from the responsible federal department, and this annex does not invent one.

**That is a Belgian echo of a German finding.** [germany-data-protection.md](germany-data-protection.md) records a Bavarian authority's checklist saying in May 2025 that the competent German authority was not yet settled. Germany settled it on 2026-07-29. Belgium has not, and the reported reason — the division of competence between the federal state and the regions — could not be verified here, because `dekamer.be` refused the automated download of the parliamentary documents that discuss it. **Recorded as blocked, not as unexamined.**

**What Belgium does have is better than most for this skill's reader.** The FOD Economie runs an AI Act information offering for businesses, organised by **what the founder actually does** — four routes: using no AI yet, using AI in the business, developing AI systems, developing general-purpose AI models. For a solo founder that is the right first question, and it is the only official national material in these packs arranged that way.

**BIPT corroborates the pack's amended dates, from outside the Regulation.** Its phased timeline gives 2025-02-02 for AI literacy and the prohibitions, 2025-08-02 for governance and **sanctions**, 2026-08-02 for non-high-risk systems and transparency supervision, **2026-12-02** for the two new prohibitions — which it describes in substance, as non-consensual sexual content and child sexual abuse material — **2027-12-02** for Annex III high-risk and **2028-08-02** for Annex I. Every shared row matches [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §5. **A second official source arriving at the same eight dates is worth more than a third reading of art. 113.**

**And one row BIPT has that the layer does not:** *"2.08.2027 — Operationaliteit van de AI-testomgeving voor regelgeving"*, the regulatory sandbox under arts. 57 and 58. Art. 57's own deadline was **[not researched]** for this layer, so the row is recorded as BIPT's and not adopted.

**The FOD's penalty answer repeats the headline and not the exception.** Its FAQ gives *"7 % van de wereldwijde jaarlijkse omzet ... of 35 miljoen euro"* for prohibited practices. [eu-ai-act.md](eu-ai-act.md) §6 records that **art. 99(6) makes an SME pay the lower of the percentage and the fixed sum, not the higher**. The FAQ does not mention it. That is not an error in the FAQ — it is why the layer quotes art. 99(6) rather than a summary.
