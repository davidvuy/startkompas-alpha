# EU — small-enterprise VAT scheme

**This is a shared layer, not a country.** Council Directive (EU) 2020/285 sets the frame every EU member state's small-business VAT exemption sits inside. The national figures differ; the ceiling on them and the cross-border rule do not.

Read this together with the relevant national pack. Neither replaces the other.

## The two thresholds the directive sets

Source: [Council Directive (EU) 2020/285, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32020L0285) · *primary EU legislation* · **[checked 2026-11-10]**

| | Figure | Provision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ceiling on a national threshold** — the most a member state may set | **€85,000** or the national-currency equivalent | art. 284(1): *"shall be no higher than EUR 85 000"* |
| **Union annual turnover** — the cap for the cross-border exemption | **€100,000** | art. 284(2)(a): *"the Union annual turnover of that taxable person does not exceed EUR 100 000"* |

Member states had to adopt the provisions by **2024-12-31** and apply them from **2025-01-01** (art. 3(1)).

## What this means in practice

**The national figure is a choice within a ceiling.** €85,000 is not anybody's threshold — it is the maximum a member state is allowed to set. That is why the four national packs show €20,000 (NL), €25,000 (BE and DE's previous-year figure), €37,500 (FR services) and €85,000 (FR sales, which sits exactly at the ceiling). A founder asking "what is the EU threshold" is asking a question with no answer: there is a ceiling, and then there is their country's figure.

**The cross-border exemption has two conditions, not one.** Union annual turnover must stay under €100,000, **and** the business must stay under the national threshold of every member state where it applies the scheme. Meeting one is not enough. The Dutch EU-KOR page states both conditions explicitly, which is consistent with the directive.

**€100,000 is an EU figure, not a national one.** Where a national pack mentions a €100,000 cross-border threshold, it is this provision. Do not treat it as that country's own invention, and do not confuse it with Germany's €100,000 **current-year domestic** figure under §19 UStG — which is an unrelated number that happens to coincide. That coincidence is a live trap: the German figure is domestic and current-year, the directive's is Union-wide and prior-year.
